What does this machine do?

An engine turns the chemical energy in fuel into heat, and then turns that heat into motion.

  1. FuelPetrol and air hold energy in their chemical bonds.
  2. FireThe spark lights the mixture; burning releases the energy as heat, very fast.
  3. PressureThe hot gas is trapped in the cylinder, so its pressure jumps to tens of bar.
  4. PushThat pressure pushes the piston down with kilonewtons of force.
  5. SpinThe rod and crankshaft turn the push into rotation (torque) that drives the wheels.

The other three strokes (intake, compression, exhaust) just get the cylinder ready for the next burn. With four cylinders firing in turn (1-3-4-2), one is always pushing, so the crank keeps turning smoothly.

The ECU produces no energy: it is the controller. It reads the sensors and decides how much fuel to inject and exactly when to fire each spark. Only about 25–35% of the fuel’s energy ends up turning the crankshaft; the rest leaves as heat through the exhaust and the cooling system.

fuel → fire → pressure → push → spin
